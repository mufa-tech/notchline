How it is worked out

The common rafter is the long side of a right triangle: the run along the bottom, the rise up the side. Its length is the run times the slope factor, √(144 + r²) ÷ 12 for a pitch of r in 12. A 6 in 12 roof has a factor of 1.1180, so a 12 ft run gives a 13 ft 5 in rafter.

Any two of the four sides and angles fix the triangle. If you type three that do not agree, the page says by how much and changes nothing.

The length is measured along the top edge of the rafter, from the building line to the ridge centre. Take off half the ridge board, measured level, before you cut.

The birdsmouth check

The seat cut sits on the wall plate; the heel cut is the plumb face above it. The notch depth, measured square to the rafter, is the seat times the sine of the pitch. The IRC (R802.7.1 pointing to R502.8.1) keeps a notch at the end of a sawn rafter within a quarter of its depth, and R802.6 asks for 1 ½ in of bearing on wood or 3 in on masonry. The page checks both.

Many carpenters use one third of the depth as a rule of thumb. The page shows it too, labelled as a rule of thumb, not a code. The structure itself, rafter size and span, is for your engineer or the span tables.

Pitch in 12ths, degrees and percent

4 in 12 is 18.4° or 33.3 %, 6 in 12 is 26.6° or 50 %, 12 in 12 is 45° or 100 %. The page converts as you switch.
